Agriculture: Bloomberg-Matched Expiry Calendar

Corn, Wheat, Soybean, Soybean Oil, Coffee, Sugar. Carry F1-F2, Value F3, Momentum 3-way MA, 5 bps costs, reporting from 2011-01-01. Prices: data/Prices_0924/Agriculture_Futures_Updated.xlsx (to 2026-09-23). Generated 2026-10-10.

Which contract months does Bloomberg use?

Each Bloomberg F-column was matched, price for price, to the individual monthly contracts on the same date. The rule "F1 = earliest contract whose last trade date is on or after the date; Fk = the k-th such contract" then rebuilt the Bloomberg series.

Result: for all six products Bloomberg uses every month the exchange lists, in order, with no skipped month and no partial cycle. Rolling happens the day after the last trade date. The rule reproduces Bloomberg exactly (100.00%) through F8 for Corn, Wheat, Soybean and Soybean Oil, through F7 for Coffee (its full depth) and through F9 for Sugar. Sugar F10-F12 differ only on 2026 dates where Bloomberg points past Oct-2028, the last contract in the monthly file; no listed contract disagrees. "Rebuilt: on LTD" is the rival roll rule (switching on the last trade date itself) and fits worse for every product.

Sugar No.11: what was wrong and what was done

The original problem

  • The existing calendar was converted from a consolidated source sheet, Expiry_Calendars_Updated_2.xlsx ("Agriculture"), whose Sugar rows were corrupted: off-cycle month codes carried the date of the neighbouring real contract back to 2005, and 16 contracts (U24 to Q26) shared one stale date, 2024-09-13. Sugar was therefore left out of the calendar and out of the agri product list.
  • That source workbook is not in the repository, so I could not re-inspect it; the description above is from the notes in the calendar file and scripts/rolling_continuous.py.

What the data shows now

  • The Sugar monthly download (SB_monthly_long.csv) is clean: 96 contracts, only the real cycle (Mar, May, Jul, Oct), no duplicate last trade dates, and none equal to 2024-09-13.
  • All 96 last trade dates equal the ICE rule, the last business day of the month before the delivery month.
  • Bloomberg's Sugar series (F1 to F12) follows that same four-month cycle and is rebuilt exactly from the monthly contracts through F9. F10-F12 differ only on 2026 dates that need contracts beyond Oct-2028, which the monthly file does not contain.

The fix

  • A Sugar sheet built straight from the monthly file's Bloomberg last trade dates: 88 contracts, Mar-2005 to Oct-2026; F1 rebuilt from the calendar matches Bloomberg on 5463 of 5463 days.
  • The loader needs a last-delivery date column, which the monthly file lacks. I filled it with the last business day of the delivery month (a derived value, not a Bloomberg field). It is not used in the roll calculations.
Impact: adding Sugar lowers the full-sample Carry IR from 0.015 to -0.155. Sugar's own F1-F2 carry IR is -0.428, the weakest of the six.

Steps performed

  1. Listed the inputs: Bloomberg curves in data/Prices_0924/Agriculture_Futures_Updated.xlsx, monthly contracts in data/monthly futures data/ (C, W, S, BO, KC, SB), and the existing Agri_Rolling_Calendar.xlsx. The data/06-30 copy of the price file is byte-identical.
  2. Investigated Sugar (see the Sugar tab).
  3. Matched every Bloomberg F-column to the monthly contracts on the same date (exact, 1e-6): matched counts by position and month, ambiguous and unmatched shares, F1 roll timing.
  4. Rebuilt Bloomberg F1..Fn from the monthly contracts under two roll rules and measured the exact-match rate.
  5. Compared the existing calendar's contracts and last trade dates with the monthly files.
  6. Built Agri_Rolling_Calendar_bloomberg_matched.xlsx (six sheets plus README and Alignment_Notes), with months taken from the match tables and last trade dates from the monthly files; asserted contract counts per year, sort order, weekdays and delivery-month lead; rebuilt F1 from the calendar itself; round-tripped it through the production loader.
  7. Built the ratio-continuous F1 series for each product with the earlier and the new calendar; they are identical for the five shared products (index, series and roll phase).
  8. Ran every strategy and portfolio method with carry F1-F2 through the project's own functions (patched in memory; no repo file changed) for variants A, B, C, and a trimmed-horizon sensitivity.
  9. Generated this page.

Things to know

  • research/run_regime_table.py does not list "agri" among its asset classes, and research/configs/agri.py has an empty carry-pair list and no Sugar. I therefore called its functions directly with in-memory settings: carry F1-F2, Sugar added, and the matched calendar. To use it from the command line later, "agri" would need adding and the config completing.
  • The roll engine treats a contract whose last trade date falls after the last price date as expiring on the last price date, so the final week of every product is rolled early (it tracks the second contract). The existing calendar has this too (Dec-2026 contracts). Trimming those contracts leaves full-sample IRs within 0.001 and moves 2026 year-to-date IRs by at most 0.02 (Carry -1.789 vs -1.770).
  • Sugar's last-delivery date is derived (see the Sugar tab).
