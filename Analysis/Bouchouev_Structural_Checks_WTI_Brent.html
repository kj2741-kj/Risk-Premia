Check #2 — 2016 Crossover Instability PARTIAL

Book (Fig 5.2/5.3): in 2016, M(1,20) lost 40%, M(3,20) made +20%, M(2,20)/M(4,20) were flat.

M(1,20) M(2,20) M(3,20) M(4,20)
What matches: the core claim — that neighboring MA-fast parameters (differing by just 1–3 days) produce wildly divergent 2016 outcomes rather than smoothly degrading — replicates cleanly on our WTI data: the four variants span +1.5% to −36.6%, a huge dispersion for such a small parameter change. What doesn't match: which variant wins/loses is different (our worst is M(3,20), his was best; our M(1,20) is bad in both, at least directionally consistent). Expected — the specific whipsaw days that break a given MA pair are a function of the exact price path, which differs by vendor/sample.

Check #5 — WTI–Brent "Accordion" STRUCTURAL MATCH

Book (Ch 6.3): Regime 1 (pre-2011) stable small premium; Regime 2 (2011–2015) spread blows to ~−$20/bbl (export-ban-driven); Regime 3 (2016–now) oscillatory/mean-reverting, Sharpe≈1.0 on his threshold-vs-60d-MA rule.

Level/shape structure matches well: Regime 1 sits near-flat (mean −$0.06), Regime 2 blows out to a mean of −$11 with a low of −$27.9 — directly in line with his ~−$20 anecdote — and Regime 3 oscillates in a tighter band (mean −$4.3, mostly −$10 to +$4) with one extreme dislocation tail. Direction matches too: the mean-reversion rule is negative Sharpe full-sample (-0.45) but flips positive when restricted to Regime 3 (+0.17) — exactly his point that this is a Regime-3-specific phenomenon, not a universal edge. Magnitude is weaker than his Sharpe≈1.0 — expected given a z-score adaptation (spreads can cross zero, so his relative-deviation formula isn't directly applicable) rather than his exact tuned rule; a candidate for follow-up tuning, not a contradiction of the structural finding.
