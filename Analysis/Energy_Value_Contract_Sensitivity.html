Energy Risk Premia — Value Contract Sensitivity

Products: WTI, Brent, RBOB, Heating Oil, Nat Gas, Singapore Gasoil, Fuel Oil (7 products, mixed NYMEX/ICE/Argus calendars). No prior Energy Value-contract-sensitivity or portfolio deep-dive report exists yet to cross-check against (unlike Metals) — the F8 baseline here was computed fresh through the identical pipeline and independently sanity-checked against a direct python research/run_regime_table.py energy run (see Section 5). This report reruns the ENTIRE pipeline — all 6 individual strategies, all 4 portfolio combine methods, full-sample/regime/expanding-window tables — three times, each time with Value's anchor contract changed to F1, then F2, then F3. Momentum, both Carry tenor pairs (F1-F3, F1-F12), and both Carry-Momentum tenor pairs are held EXACTLY as locked in research/configs/energy.py — only VALUE_CONTRACT changes. Lookback (1260d), threshold (±10%), and execution lag (shift_n=2) are unchanged from the F8 baseline in every variant.

1. Overview

Value is a long-run mean-reversion signal: it compares one point on the futures curve to that same point's own trailing moving average, and bets the price reverts toward that average when the deviation is large enough to clear a threshold. The baseline uses F8 as the anchor, deliberately further from the front of the curve than Momentum and Carry (both of which operate on F1). Moving the anchor to F1, F2, or F3 asks whether the front/near price mean-reverts to its own 5-year moving average instead — a shorter-horizon-feeling question than F8's curve-structural read, even though the lookback window (1260 trading days) and threshold (±10%) stay identical.

1.1 What's held fixed across all four variants (F8 baseline, F1, F2, F3)

  • Momentum: 3-way MA-crossover composite, (1,20)/(5,60)/(20,250), shift_n=1
  • Carry V1(Level)+V2(Z-score) composite: F1-F3 and F1-F12 tenor pairs (Energy's own two-tier structure — F12 not F13, since Fuel Oil has no F13 column and its own F12 is only 48% quoted, weaker than the other six products' Carry/CarryMom legs), z-window=252, shift_n=1
  • Carry-Momentum (V3): F1-F3 and F1-F12 tenor pairs, horizon=20, shift_n=1
  • Value lookback (1260d), threshold (±10%), execution lag (shift_n=2) — only the anchor CONTRACT changes
  • Transaction cost: 5bps round-trip. Products: WTI, Brent, RBOB, Heating Oil, Nat Gas, Singapore Gasoil, Fuel Oil, equal-weighted. Regimes: R1 Supercycle Unwind (2011-2015), R2 Recovery/Stabilization (2016-2019), R3 COVID Shock (2020-2021), R4 Post-COVID/Russia-Ukraine (2022-2026). Portfolio: the 6-sleeve "all reference strategies" aggregate (Momentum, Carry F1-F3, Carry F1-F12, CarryMom F1-F3, CarryMom F1-F12, Value) — Equal Weight, Inverse Vol (63d), Risk Parity (ERC, rolling 252d/21d rebal), and Return-Tilt ERC (tilt=0.5, illustrative midpoint), same construction confirmed correct for Metals.

Expanding-window tables use the same convention as the Metals report: cumulative-sample performance snapshots through each regime boundary (2011→2015, 2011→2019, 2011→2021, 2011→latest), NOT a train/test split — these are fixed ex-ante rule-based signals, nothing is refit between windows.

StatArb note: research/configs/energy.py has STATARB_ENABLED = True (Bogorad's own 8-spread cross-asset-class sleeve, shared with NGL), but this report is built on research/run_regime_table.py's pipeline, which does not include StatArb regardless of that flag — it only ever builds Momentum/Carry/CarryMom/Value. So, as with the Metals report, this covers those 6 strategies only.

1.2 Inverse Vol data-quality caveat (read before Section 2)

Unlike Metals, Energy's Inverse Vol combine method produces no valid full-sample result at any Value-contract variant, including the F8 baseline — see Section 6 for the full explanation (Energy's mixed NYMEX/ICE/Argus exchange calendars create a ~9.8% gap-day rate that defeats combine_returns's strict 63-day rolling-window requirement). Inverse Vol rows below are marked † and shown as N/A.

6. Findings / synthesis

Moving Value's anchor toward the front of the curve slightly IMPROVES Value's own standalone IR, but the effect is much smaller than in Metals and does not flip the sign: F8 -0.089 → F1 -0.087 → F2 -0.065 → F3 -0.064. Value stays net-negative full-sample in EVERY variant for Energy — unlike Metals, where Value was the strongest individual strategy at every contract tested, Energy's Value signal never clears zero on its own regardless of anchor.

Regime pattern is broadly stable: Value is negative in R1 (Supercycle Unwind, worst regime in every variant) and R3 (COVID Shock), positive in R2 (Recovery/Stabilization) and R4 (Post-COVID/Russia-Ukraine), across F8 and all three front-curve variants — the sign pattern by regime does not change with the anchor contract, only the magnitude shifts modestly.

Portfolio-level conclusion is very different from Metals: Energy's 6-strategy portfolio is net POSITIVE in every variant, at every combine method that produces a valid number (Equal Weight and both ERC-based methods) — Equal Weight ranges +0.628 (F8) to +0.644 (F1); Risk Parity (ERC) ranges +0.654 to +0.710; Return-Tilt ERC ranges +0.662 to +0.676. This is the opposite finding from Metals (where no combine method cleared zero at the F8 baseline, and only Risk Parity/Return-Tilt ERC crossed positive at some front-curve variants) — Energy's portfolio is carried by Momentum and CarryMom (F1-F12), both strongly IR-positive full-sample, not by Value, which stays a drag in every variant tested.

Value-contract choice barely moves the Energy portfolio conclusion: since Value's own IR change across F8/F1/F2/F3 is small (about 0.02-0.03 in absolute IR terms) and Value is not the dominant sleeve in this asset class's portfolio the way it is in Metals, the portfolio-level IR for Equal Weight/ERC/Return-Tilt ERC moves by only a few hundredths across all four contract choices — nowhere near enough to change which combine method looks best or whether the portfolio is net-positive.

Data-quality caveat — Inverse Vol produces no valid result for Energy, in the F8 baseline as well as every F1/F2/F3 variant. Energy mixes three different exchange calendars across its 7 products (NYMEX: WTI/RBOB/HeatingOil/NatGas; ICE: Brent/SingaporeGasoil; Argus-style: FuelOil) — roughly 24-30 trading days a year (about 9.8% of all days, evenly spread across 2006-2026, not concentrated in any one period) exist on some products' calendars but not others', which per engine.py::combine_returns's own documented convention leaves every one of the six strategy-level return series with a NaN gap on those specific dates (same ~524 dates across all six, since the gap comes from the product-combining step every strategy shares). This does not affect Equal Weight or the two ERC-based methods (Equal Weight's _metrics() call drops NaN rows before computing; rolling_erc_combine explicitly drops any row with a NaN in ANY series before its covariance solve, shrinking the effective sample by ~10% rather than propagating NaN). But Inverse Vol's rolling-63-day volatility estimate uses min_periods=vol_window (all 63 days must be valid) — with a ~9.8% gap-day rate spread evenly through the sample, essentially no 63-day trailing window across the entire 2006-2026 history is ever completely gap-free (verified directly: 100% of rolling windows contain at least one gap day), so Inverse Vol's combined series is NaN on every single day, for every Value-contract variant including the F8 baseline. This is a genuine, pre-existing limitation of the current combine_returns(..., "inverse_vol") implementation as applied to Energy specifically (Metals/Precious Metals are unaffected, sharing one calendar each per engine.py's own docstring) — not something introduced by this sensitivity study, and not specific to Value's contract choice. It is disclosed here rather than silently omitted or worked around, since fixing it would require changing the locked combine_returns function itself, which is out of scope for this report.
