1. Overview

Value is a long-run mean-reversion signal: it compares one point on the futures curve to that same point's own trailing moving average, and bets the price reverts toward that average when the deviation is large enough to clear a threshold. The baseline uses F8 as the anchor, deliberately further from the front of the curve than Momentum and Carry (both of which operate on F1). Moving the anchor to F1, F2, or F3 asks whether the front/near price mean-reverts to its own 5-year moving average instead — a shorter-horizon-feeling question than F8's curve-structural read, even though the lookback window (1260 trading days) and threshold (±10%) stay identical. NONE of this section changed today — only Carry's second tenor pair did (see 1.1).

6. Findings / synthesis

Moving Value's anchor toward the front of the curve slightly IMPROVES Value's own standalone IR, and the effect remains small and never flips the sign: F8 -0.089 → F1 -0.087 → F2 -0.065 → F3 -0.064. Value stays net-negative full-sample in EVERY variant for Energy, same conclusion as the F1-F12 report — this asset class's Value signal never clears zero on its own regardless of anchor, and switching Carry's second tenor pair to F1-F13 does not change that.

Regime pattern is unchanged from the F1-F12 report: Value is negative in R1 (Supercycle Unwind) and R3 (COVID Shock), positive in R2 (Recovery/Stabilization) and R4 (Post-COVID/Russia-Ukraine), across F8 and all three front-curve variants — the Carry tenor change does not touch Value's own computation at all (Value never references the Carry tenor pair), so this regime pattern is identical to before by construction.

Portfolio-level conclusion holds: Energy's 6-strategy portfolio (now with Carry F1-F13 instead of F1-F12) is still net POSITIVE in every variant, at every combine method that produces a valid number (Equal Weight and both ERC-based methods) — Equal Weight ranges +0.624 to +0.644; Risk Parity (ERC) ranges +0.639 to +0.703; Return-Tilt ERC ranges +0.641 to +0.681. Energy's portfolio is still carried by Momentum and CarryMom (now F1-F13), both strongly IR-positive full-sample, not by Value.

Carry (F1-F13) itself is modestly WEAKER than the old Carry (F1-F12): full-sample IR +0.394 at F1-F13 vs the old report's +0.418 at F1-F12 — a real, small degradation, plausibly related to Fuel Oil's F1-F13 leg being silently flat (no F13 column for that product at all, see the caveat in Section 1.1), which the F1-F12 pair did not suffer from (Fuel Oil's own F12 was at least 48% quoted, weak but non-zero). CarryMom (F1-F13) is essentially unchanged (+0.615 vs the old +0.609), and the portfolio-level combine-method numbers only move by a few hundredths overall — not enough to change which combine method looks best or whether the portfolio stays net-positive.

NEW caveat for this Carry-tenor version: Fuel Oil has no F13 column at all (only 12 tenors total), so its Carry (F1-F13) and CarryMom (F1-F13) contribution is silently flat/zero every day (_carry_base() returns an empty series when a requested tenor column is missing, which reindexes to an all-zero position, not a crash) — diluting the reported 7-product average by one always-flat weight for those two strategies specifically. This is a research-pipeline-only artifact: the live dashboard's Portfolio tab excludes Fuel Oil entirely (a separate, earlier change today), so it never sees this dilution.

Data-quality caveat — Inverse Vol still produces no valid result for Energy, in the F8 baseline as well as every F1/F2/F3 variant, unchanged from the F1-F12 report. Energy mixes three different exchange calendars across its 7 products (NYMEX: WTI/RBOB/HeatingOil/NatGas; ICE: Brent/SingaporeGasoil; Argus-style: FuelOil) — roughly 9.8% of all days exist on some products' calendars but not others', which leaves every strategy-level return series with a NaN gap on those dates. Equal Weight and both ERC-based methods drop NaN rows before computing; Inverse Vol's rolling-63-day volatility estimate requires all 63 days valid (min_periods=vol_window), and with a ~9.8% gap rate spread evenly through the sample, essentially no 63-day window is ever completely gap-free, so Inverse Vol's combined series is NaN on every single day. This is a pre-existing limitation of combine_returns(..., "inverse_vol") as applied to Energy specifically, unrelated to the Carry tenor change and unrelated to Value's contract choice — confirmed still present after this recompute (checked directly: True).

7. Combined Carry — alternative portfolio construction

Added 2026-08-04. Sections 1–6 above treat each of Energy's two Carry tenor pairs (F1-F3, F1-F13) as its own separate row, combining all six sleeves (Momentum, Carry F1-F3, Carry F1-F13, CarryMom F1-F3, CarryMom F1-F13, Value) directly. Dimil (project collaborator) built an alternative convention in Research_Dashboard_CombinedCarry.html, reproduced here for Energy the same way it was for Metals: first equal-weight the two Carry tenor pairs into one Combined Carry row, and the two CarryMom variants into one Combined CarryMom row, then combine exactly four rows (Momentum, Combined Carry, Combined CarryMom, Value) — a cleaner "4 main risk premia" framing. This section extends that construction to all 4 combine methods used elsewhere in this report (Dimil's own dashboard showed Equal Weight and Risk Parity only). Value on F3 (today's actual default) — consistent with the rest of this report, which already runs on F3 as its live tab (Sections 2–6 tab between F1/F2/F3; F8 appears only as the historical baseline column in Section 5's comparison tables).

This is an additive, alternative presentation, not a correction — Sections 1–6's six-row numbers remain the officially reported ones for this report. Both conventions are internally consistent; they simply weight the underlying strategies differently (six-row: each tenor pair gets 1/6 weight directly; four-row: each tenor pair gets 1/8 weight via two levels of 1/2-then-1/4 averaging, while Momentum and Value each get 1/4 instead of 1/6). The Fuel Oil no-F13-column caveat (Section 1.1) and the Inverse Vol data-quality caveat (Section 1.2) both carry over unchanged into this section — Combined Carry and Combined CarryMom still silently dilute Fuel Oil's F1-F13 leg to flat/zero, and Inverse Vol is still N/A for every table below.

7.7 Findings

Unlike Metals, Momentum — not Value — is the strongest individual sleeve under the 4-row Combined Carry construction (full-sample IR +0.586), followed closely by Combined CarryMom (+0.541); Combined Carry is more modest (+0.348), and Value is the only row that stays net-negative full-sample (-0.064), consistent with Sections 1-6's own finding that Energy's Value signal never clears zero on its own regardless of anchor contract.
Every combine method that produces a valid number (Equal Weight, Risk Parity, Return-Tilt ERC) stays solidly positive full-sample and clusters tightly, +0.657 to +0.686 — a real contrast with Metals, where the four-row combine spanned -0.095 to +0.063 and never cleared zero. For Energy, the 4-row vs 6-row weighting choice barely moves the portfolio number at all: the six-row report's F3 full-sample figures (Section 5) are +0.642 (Equal Weight), +0.683 (Risk Parity), +0.681 (Return-Tilt ERC) — within a few thousandths of this section's four-row numbers, because Combined Carry/CarryMom's averaging doesn't change which underlying sleeves (Momentum, CarryMom) are actually driving the portfolio.
R3 (COVID Shock) is the standout regime everywhere in this section, same as in Sections 1-6: Momentum peaks at +1.394, Combined CarryMom at +0.892, and the Risk Parity portfolio hits its sample-wide high of +1.598 — consistent with Energy's well-documented 2020 dislocation (WTI's brief negative-price event) driving outsized trend and spread moves across the whole asset class, not something specific to the Combined Carry framing.
