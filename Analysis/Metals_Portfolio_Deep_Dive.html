Metals Risk Premia — Portfolio Deep Dive

Products: Copper, Aluminium, Lead, Zinc (LME). Common analysis window: 2006-01-03 to 2026-06-30. Reporting window (post-burn-in): 2011-01-01 onward. Transaction cost: 5bps round-trip. Methodology: log-return PnL on a ratio-adjusted continuous F1 price, ex-ante fixed-parameter signals (nothing fitted/optimized on data).

1. Overview & Data 2. Regime Definitions 3. Signal Methodology (with worked Copper examples) 4. Portfolio Construction (4 combine methods) 5. Results (Equal Weight / Inverse Vol / ERC / Return-Tilt ERC) 6. Findings & Synthesis

1. Overview & Data

The Metals dashboard covers four LME base metals — Copper, Aluminium, Lead, Zinc — using the F1..F27 forward curve (raw price levels, used for Carry and Value) and a roll-aware continuous F1 series (used for Momentum and for computing realized PnL). Two continuous-price conventions exist in this project: an additively roll-adjusted F1_continuous (used by the live Streamlit dashboards, can go negative, fine for single-product dollar PnL) and a ratio-adjusted F1_continuous_ratio (used here and throughout this research pipeline, always positive, safe to take logs of and combine across products/asset classes). All results below use the log-return methodology on the ratio-adjusted series.

Each product's signals are computed on its own full available history (so a longer-lookback signal like Value's 1260-day moving average gets maximum warm-up), and only the resulting per-product NET return series are aligned to the common cross-product window (2006-01-03 to 2026-06-30) before combining. Reporting starts 2011-01-01 across every asset class in this project, dropping 2006–2010 entirely — that period sits inside or overlaps the burn-in window for the longest-lookback signals, so by 2011-01-01 every signal in every asset class is on equal, fully-warmed-up footing.

Transaction costs: 5bps round-trip, charged on position changes (half the round-trip rate per one-directional change) plus a roll-day charge for positions held through a contract roll. Execution is lagged (signal decided at close t, position becomes live at t+1 or later depending on the strategy's shift parameter) — no same-bar look-ahead anywhere.

StatArb is deliberately NOT part of the Metals dashboard. It was tested rigorously across 4 specifications (raw levels full-sample, log-prices full-sample, raw-levels regime-split, macro-factor-adjusted regime-split) on all 6 candidate pairs, including Zinc–Lead (the strongest a priori economic story, both being base metals with correlated industrial demand). The result was a genuine null across every specification and every pair — no cointegration/mean-reversion edge survived. This is a tested-and-rejected finding, not an oversight, so Metals runs on Momentum + Carry + Value only.

Four macro regimes, locked ex-ante and shared across every asset class in this project (not re-fit per asset class):

These are the same R1–R4 labels the user's regime-analysis work uses elsewhere in this project; a fifth "2026 YTD" slice is tracked separately in the underlying data but folded into R4 here since R4 is already open-ended.

3. Signal Methodology

Three strategy families, all ex-ante parameter-locked (no fitting/optimization on data) — the numbers in each example below use real Copper data on 2018-06-15, a date chosen only for having full history available for every signal's longest lookback (Value's 1260 trading days).

3.1 Momentum — 3-way moving-average crossover composite

For each of three (fast, slow) lookback pairs — (1,20), (5,60), (20,250) trading days — take the sign of (fast MA − slow MA) on the raw continuous F1 price. Average the three raw signs, then take the sign of that average as the final composite signal. Averaging smooths "which lookback is right" fragility since all three pairs measure the same underlying hypothesis (price trend) at different speeds.

signal_pair(fast,slow)[t] = sign( MA_fast(F1)[t] − MA_slow(F1)[t] )
composite[t] = sign( mean( signal_(1,20)[t], signal_(5,60)[t], signal_(20,250)[t] ) )
position[t] = composite[t − 2]  (1-day execution lag + 1-day extra shift)

Worked example — Copper, 2018-06-15

Pair (fast,slow)MA fastMA slowFast − SlowSign
(1,20)7019.507027.65-8.15-1 (short)
(5,60)7184.356880.75+303.60+1 (long)
(20,250)7027.656728.66+298.99+1 (long)

F1 raw price on 2018-06-15: 7019.50. Average of the three signs = +0.333 → composite sign = +1 (long). Two pairs vote long, one votes short — the majority (and the average's sign) determines the trade.

3.2 Carry V1 (Level) and V2 (Z-score)

Both derived from the same raw carry slope on one tenor pair (e.g. F1 vs F3): slope = (F_near − F_far) / F_near. A positive slope means the curve is in backwardation (near month priced above far month) — the classic "carry" signal takes this as bullish (go long) since backwardation historically compensates a long futures position via positive roll yield. V1 trades the raw sign of that slope directly; V2 instead z-scores the slope against its own trailing 252-day mean/std before taking the sign, so V2 reacts to whether carry is unusually steep/flat relative to its OWN recent history, not its absolute level. The locked ex-ante Carry anchor averages V1 and V2 (not V3/Carry-Momentum, a different hypothesis kept separate — see 3.3).

slope[t] = (F_near[t] − F_far[t]) / F_near[t]
V1_sign[t] = sign( slope[t] )
z[t] = ( slope[t] − rollmean_252(slope)[t] ) / rollstd_252(slope)[t]
V2_sign[t] = sign( z[t] )
composite[t] = sign( (V1_sign[t] + V2_sign[t]) / 2 )

Worked example — Copper F1–F3, 2018-06-15

F1 = 7019.50, F3 = 7015.00 → slope = (7019.50 − 7015.00) / 7019.50 = +0.000641 (+0.0641%) → V1 sign = +1 (long) (slightly backwardated curve here, i.e. F1 above F3).

252-day rolling mean of the slope = -0.003308, rolling std = 0.001058 → z = (+0.000641 − -0.003308) / 0.001058 = +3.731 → V2 sign = +1 (long). Here the market has been persistently in contango over the trailing year (negative mean slope), so today's mild backwardation is a large positive surprise (z ≈ 3.7 standard deviations) — both V1 and V2 agree long, composite = +1.

3.3 Carry-Momentum (V3) — rate-of-change on the carry slope

Kept as its own separate strategy, not blended into the V1/V2 Carry composite: this is a momentum-on-carry construct (is the curve steepening or flattening?), a different economic hypothesis from V1/V2's level-based signal. Takes the sign of the change in the carry slope over a fixed horizon (20 trading days).

diff[t] = slope[t] − slope[t − 20]
signal[t] = sign( diff[t] )

Worked example — Copper F1–F3, 2018-06-15

Slope today = +0.000641; slope 20 trading days ago = -0.002695 → diff = +0.003336 → sign = +1 (long). The curve moved from meaningfully contango 20 days ago to mildly backwardated today — a steepening-toward-backwardation move, which Carry-Momentum reads as bullish.

3.4 Value — long-run mean-reversion on F8

Compares today's F8 (a mid-curve, ~8-month-forward contract, chosen as the dashboard's long-standing anchor) against its own 1260-trading-day (~5 year) moving average. If price has fallen more than 10% below that long-run average, Value goes long (expecting reversion up); if it has risen more than 10% above, Value goes short; inside the ±10% band it stays flat.

MA[t] = rollmean_1260( F8 )[t]
dev[t] = ( F8[t] − MA[t] ) / MA[t]
signal[t] = +1 if dev[t] < −10%, −1 if dev[t] > +10%, else 0

Worked example — Copper F8, 2018-06-15

F8 = 7052.00, 1260-day MA = 6093.16 → deviation = (7052.00 − 6093.16) / 6093.16 = +15.74%. This is above the +10% threshold, so Value signals -1 (short) — price is running well above its 5-year average, so Value fades it (expects mean reversion down).

4. Portfolio Construction — 4 Combine Methods

All four methods below combine the SAME six strategy-level net-return series (Momentum, Carry F1-F3, Carry F1-F13, CarryMom F1-F3, CarryMom F1-F13, Value — each itself already an equal-weight blend across the four products) into one portfolio return series. They differ only in how much weight each of the six sleeves gets on a given day.

4.1 Equal Weight

The simplest baseline: every sleeve gets weight 1/N regardless of its volatility, correlation, or recent performance.

portfolio[t] = mean( sleeve_1[t], sleeve_2[t], ..., sleeve_6[t] )

4.2 Inverse Vol

Reweights daily so lower-volatility sleeves get more weight, ignoring cross-sleeve correlation entirely. Each sleeve's weight is 1 / its own trailing 63-day realized vol, renormalized to sum to 1 across the six sleeves that day. This is a rolling, look-ahead-free scheme (day t's weights use only strictly-prior returns via pandas' own rolling-window semantics).

w_i[t] = (1 / vol_63d(sleeve_i)[t]) / sum_j (1 / vol_63d(sleeve_j)[t])
portfolio[t] = sum_i w_i[t] * sleeve_i[t]

4.3 Risk Parity (ERC, rolling)

Solves for the weight vector under which every sleeve contributes EQUAL risk to total portfolio variance — unlike Inverse Vol, this accounts for cross-sleeve correlation via the full covariance matrix, not just each sleeve's own vol. No closed-form solution exists in general, so this solves the convex reformulation of Maillard, Roncalli & Teiletche (2010):

minimize 0.5 * y' Σ y − sum_i (1/N) * log(y_i) subject to y > 0
w = y / sum(y)

Σ is the trailing 252-day covariance matrix of the six sleeves' returns, re-estimated and the portfolio rebalanced every 21 trading days (~monthly), using only data strictly before the rebalance date. Before the first full 252-day window is available, falls back to equal weight.

4.4 Return-Tilt ERC

Same rolling-ERC mechanism as 4.3, but blends the uniform risk budget with one proportional to each sleeve's trailing Sharpe ratio (same 252-day window). A tunable parameter tilt ∈ [0,1] interpolates between the two: tilt=0 is pure ERC, tilt=1 is fully performance-proportional. Every raw trailing Sharpe is floored at 0.05 before use, so even a currently weak sleeve keeps a small non-zero risk claim — even at tilt=1 this stays a diversified portfolio, not a concentrated bet on whichever sleeve happened to have the best trailing window.

budget_i = (1−tilt) * (1/N) + tilt * ( max(Sharpe_i, 0.05) / sum_j max(Sharpe_j, 0.05) )
y = argmin 0.5 * y' Σ y − sum_i budget_i * log(y_i), w = y / sum(y)
The live Metals dashboard exposes tilt as a 0–1 slider, default 0 (pure ERC). The results below use tilt = 0.5 as an illustrative midpoint to show what the tilt mechanism does — this is not a locked ex-ante project parameter, just a representative choice for this report.

6. Findings & Synthesis

Value is the only individually robust strategy. Full-sample IR +0.517, and positive in 3 of 4 regimes (R1 +0.45, R2 +0.75, R4 +0.95), only breaking down in R3/COVID (−0.54) — unsurprising, since a sharp demand-shock crash followed by a V-shaped recovery is close to a worst case for a pure mean-reversion-to-5-year-average signal that doesn't distinguish "cheap because of a temporary shock" from "cheap and staying cheap."
Momentum is the weakest individual strategy over the full sample (IR −0.433) and behaves almost as Value's mirror image regime-by-regime: negative in R1, R2, and R4 (−0.61, −0.62, −0.63), but the single best strategy-regime combination in the whole table is Momentum in R3/COVID (+0.814) — trend-following captured the COVID crash-and-recovery precisely where Value was hurt worst. This is the main diversification logic holding the portfolio together, not a redundant pair.
All four Carry variants decay from strongly positive in R1 to persistently negative afterward, and this shows up cleanly in the expanding-window table: Carry F1-F3's cumulative IR falls from +0.75 (Window A, thru R1) to +0.12 (Window B, thru R2) to about flat (Window C, thru R3) to slightly negative (Window D, full sample). The R1 Supercycle-Unwind regime (broad backwardation as the 2000s supercycle unwound) was evidently a uniquely favorable carry environment for Metals that has not recurred in the 10+ years since — this reads as a genuine regime-dependence finding, not noise, since the decay is monotonic across all four expanding windows and consistent across both tenor pairs (F1-F3 and F1-F13) and both Carry variants (V1/V2 composite and Carry-Momentum).
No combine method turns a negative full-sample portfolio positive — Equal Weight IR −0.117, Inverse Vol −0.019, Risk Parity (ERC) −0.079, Return-Tilt ERC(0.5) −0.018. Risk-based weighting (Inverse Vol, ERC, Return-Tilt) meaningfully improves on naive Equal Weight — roughly halving the loss and cutting max drawdown from −46.8% to the −33% to −38% range — but cannot overcome the arithmetic that 5 of the 6 sleeves (everything except Value) have negative full-sample IR. Inverse Vol and Return-Tilt ERC land within a rounding error of each other (−0.019 vs −0.018) and are the two best combine methods here; pure ERC sits slightly behind, likely because it weights toward low-covariance combinations without any performance information at all, and in this six-sleeve set the lowest-vol sleeves are not necessarily the best performers.
Expanding-window portfolio IR is NOT stable — every combine method shows the identical pattern: strongly positive through R1 alone (Equal Weight +0.61, Inverse Vol +0.73, ERC +0.64, Return-Tilt +0.70), collapsing to roughly flat or slightly negative by Window B (2011–2019) and staying there through Windows C and D. This mirrors the Carry decay above almost exactly (Carry is 4 of the 6 sleeves), and means a live investor reading only a "since-2011 Sharpe" headline number would be looking at a figure heavily front-loaded by 2011–2015 performance that has not been replicated since. This is the single most important caveat to carry into any Metals-based capital-allocation decision.
