Metals Risk Premia — Value Contract Sensitivity

Products: Copper, Aluminium, Lead, Zinc (LME). Baseline (reported elsewhere, see Metals_Portfolio_Deep_Dive.html): Value anchored on F8. This report reruns the ENTIRE pipeline — all 6 individual strategies, all 4 portfolio combine methods, full-sample/regime/expanding-window tables — three more times, each time with Value's anchor contract changed to F1, then F2, then F3. Momentum, both Carry tenor pairs (F1-F3, F1-F13), and both Carry-Momentum tenor pairs are held EXACTLY as locked in research/configs/metals.py — only VALUE_CONTRACT changes. Lookback (1260d), threshold (±10%), and execution lag (shift_n=2) are unchanged from the F8 baseline in every variant.

1. Overview

Value is a long-run mean-reversion signal: it compares one point on the futures curve to that same point's own trailing moving average, and bets the price reverts toward that average when the deviation is large enough to clear a threshold. The baseline uses F8 (roughly the 8th listed futures contract on the curve) as the anchor, deliberately further from the front of the curve than Momentum and Carry (which both operate on F1, the front/near contract) — the idea being that a further-out point reflects a more structural, less noisy "fair value" than the front-month price, which carries near-term supply/demand and roll noise.

Moving the anchor to F1, F2, or F3 changes the economic bet: F1 is the literal front-month price, so "Value on F1" is asking whether the SPOT/near price mean-reverts to its own 5-year moving average — a different, shorter-horizon-feeling question than F8's curve-structural read, even though the lookback window (1260 trading days) and threshold (±10%) stay identical. F2/F3 sit in between. Since F1/F2/F3 typically trade closer together and closer to spot than F8, the deviation series for these three variants will tend to be more correlated with each other than any of them is with F8, and (because the front of a metals curve is usually the most liquid, most information-dense point) potentially noisier around each threshold crossing.

1.1 What's held fixed across all four variants (F8 baseline, F1, F2, F3)

  • Momentum: 3-way MA-crossover composite, (1,20)/(5,60)/(20,250), shift_n=1
  • Carry V1(Level)+V2(Z-score) composite: F1-F3 and F1-F13 tenor pairs, z-window=252, shift_n=1
  • Carry-Momentum (V3): F1-F3 and F1-F13 tenor pairs, horizon=20, shift_n=1
  • Value lookback (1260d), threshold (±10%), execution lag (shift_n=2) — only the anchor CONTRACT changes
  • Transaction cost: 5bps round-trip. Products: Copper, Aluminium, Lead, Zinc, equal-weighted. Regimes: R1 Supercycle Unwind (2011-2015), R2 Recovery/Stabilization (2016-2019), R3 COVID Shock (2020-2021), R4 Post-COVID/Russia-Ukraine (2022-2026). Portfolio: the SAME 6-sleeve "all reference strategies" aggregate confirmed as the correct construction in the original report — Equal Weight, Inverse Vol (63d), Risk Parity (ERC, rolling 252d/21d rebal), and Return-Tilt ERC (tilt=0.5, illustrative midpoint) combining the six strategy-level series (Momentum, Carry F1-F3, Carry F1-F13, CarryMom F1-F3, CarryMom F1-F13, Value).

Expanding-window tables here use the same convention as the original report: cumulative-sample performance snapshots through each regime boundary (2011→2015, 2011→2019, 2011→2021, 2011→latest), NOT a train/test split — these are fixed ex-ante rule-based signals, nothing is refit between windows.

6. Findings / synthesis

Moving Value's anchor toward the front of the curve modestly IMPROVES Value's own standalone IR: F1 (0.626) > F2 (0.597) > F3 (0.571) > F8 (0.517) — a small, monotonic improvement as the anchor moves closer to spot. The gap is real but not large (about +0.11 IR from F8 to F1), and the ranking is monotonic across all three intermediate points, which reads as a genuine (if modest) curve-position effect rather than noise.

The regime pattern does NOT change: in every variant (F1/F2/F3, same as F8), Value is positive in R1, R2, and R4, and negative in R3 (COVID Shock) — the one regime where its mean-reversion bet consistently fails is unchanged by which contract it anchors on. Momentum's COVID-regime strength (the diversification story from the original report) is untouched since Momentum's own construction never changed.

The portfolio-level conclusion from the original report mostly holds, but with one real exception that depends on WHICH combine method you look at — these two do not behave the same way and should not be read together. Equal Weight and Inverse Vol stay negative in every variant (Equal Weight: -0.090 at F1, -0.097 at F2, -0.104 at F3, vs -0.117 at F8; Inverse Vol: -0.003 at F1, -0.017 at F2, -0.022 at F3, vs -0.019 at F8) — for these two methods, nothing crosses zero anywhere.

Return-Tilt ERC (tilt=0.5) is positive at ALL THREE of F1, F2, and F3 — +0.074, +0.041, and +0.026 respectively — and only the F8 baseline (-0.018) stays negative for this method. This is the more consistent, more repeatable finding: moving Value's anchor anywhere from F8 toward the front of the curve is enough to flip this specific combine method positive, not just at one contract but across the board.

Pure Risk Parity / ERC (tilt=0), by contrast, only crosses positive at F1 (+0.033) and stays negative at both F2 (-0.001, essentially flat) and F3 (-0.024), as well as at the F8 baseline (-0.079). So the two risk-based methods diverge under this sensitivity test: Return-Tilt ERC's positive crossing is robust across all three front-curve variants, while pure ERC's positive crossing is fragile and specific to F1 alone. Either way, this is the first result across both reports where a Metals portfolio combine method is unambiguously net-positive full-sample — worth flagging on its own, with the Return-Tilt/pure-ERC distinction kept intact.

Caveat: this crossing is driven by Value's own IR improvement (0.517 → 0.626) compounding with Risk-Parity/Return-Tilt's already-better-than-EW risk allocation — it is not a new diversification effect from changing Value's contract, since Value's correlation structure with the other 5 sleeves is barely affected by this change (F1/F2/F3 are close together on the curve). Treat the F1 result as "the best of four highly-related choices," not as evidence that front-month Value is a structurally different, better-diversifying signal.
