# Multi-Asset Systematic Risk Premia — A Macro Read on the Regime Data

*Prepared from the Research Dashboard (Metals / Oil-Energy / Precious Metals / NGL). Reporting window revised 2026-07-24: every complex now reports from 2011-01-01 onward, so all four sleeves' longest-lookback signals (Value's 1260-day moving average in particular) are fully warmed up before the reported window begins — moving averages/z-scores still use full history back to each asset's own data start, only the reported/scored window changed. Regimes: R1 Supercycle Unwind (2011–15), R2 Recovery/Stabilization (2016–19), R3 COVID Shock (2020–21), R4 Post-COVID/Russia-Ukraine (2022–26), plus 2026 YTD. This note reads the numbers as a macro trader would — what regime produced this result, does the pattern repeat across complexes, and is it a strategy edge or a macro accident.*

---

## Cross-Asset Synthesis — The Pattern Across All Four Complexes

1. **The single biggest finding of this revised analysis is what disappeared, not what's new.** Excluding 2006–2010 (which included the 2008-09 crisis) materially weakens three of the four complexes' blended full-sample results: Metals' EW Portfolio flips from +0.30 to **-0.12**; Precious Metals' from +0.16 to **-0.07**. Much of what looked like a persistent, always-on multi-strategy edge in the original numbers was concentrated in one historical crisis window that this cleaner sample no longer includes.

2. **Only two complexes still show a genuinely positive, live full-sample risk premium on 2011-2026: Energy (EW Portfolio IR 0.63) and NGL (EW Portfolio IR 1.29).** Both are backed by an ongoing structural story — Energy's post-2022 supply-security repricing, NGL's broadly positive performance across nearly every regime since 2011 — not a historical accident.

3. **Diversification is no longer a reliable free lunch across the board.** It still clearly works in Energy and NGL, where the underlying strategies have real, independent edges to combine. In Metals and Precious Metals, blending four largely weak-to-negative legs just produces a smoother weak-to-negative number — equal-weighting cannot manufacture an edge that isn't present in the components.

4. **Value (mean-reversion) is now the most durable single strategy across the two weakest complexes.** It's the only positive full-sample strategy in Metals (IR 0.52) and the strongest full-sample standalone strategy in Precious Metals (IR 0.16, narrowly ahead of Carry). In complexes without a clear structural tailwind, a five-year reversion anchor has held up better than directional or curve-based signals.

5. **Metals' Carry-family strategies show a genuine "before and after 2016" split that the old full-sample numbers hid.** They were strongly positive through the 2011-2015 supercycle unwind (Carry F1-F3 IR 0.75, CarryMom F1-F3 IR 0.77) even as prices fell — carry captures curve shape, not price direction — but have been negative in every regime since. That reads as a decaying edge, not a strategy that never worked.

6. **The StatArb sleeve's statistical case has weakened materially.** Under the old sample, the equal-weight combination's full-sample result survived Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction; on 2011-2026 alone, **zero cells survive out of 45 tested**. The directional numbers (EW StatArb full-sample IR 0.73) are still respectable, but the paper should now present this sleeve as a suggestive, not statistically confirmed, edge.

7. **2026 YTD strength stands out even more starkly against the weaker full-sample baselines.** Metals CarryMom (IR 2.34), Energy across the board (up to 2.67), Precious Metals (up to 2.30), and NGL Value (3.13, the single highest reading in the entire dataset) are all running far above their own 2011-2026 averages — a genuinely unusual, broad, positive commodity regime in H1 2026, not "normal" performance continuing.

8. **The regime-matched comparison is now clean across all four complexes, with no burn-in gaps anywhere.** Every sleeve reports on the identical 2011-2026 window on equal footing — this is what makes findings like Metals' post-2016 carry decay, or the sobering StatArb FDR result, visible for the first time rather than averaged away.

9. **Precious Metals' safe-haven decoupling is harder to see with 2008-09 excluded, but the mechanism still shows up around COVID.** PM's Momentum, Carry, and Value are all negative in the COVID Shock regime, the one window where Metals and Energy trend/carry strategies did well — the same "PM doesn't move with the rest of the cyclical complex" pattern, visible in a different crisis this time.

10. **Bottom line for the paper: this is a genuinely weaker, more honest picture of the framework's live edge than the original numbers suggested.** Energy and NGL currently justify their weight on strong, ongoing structural tailwinds. Metals and Precious Metals do not currently show a reliable blended-strategy edge — Value is the more defensible standalone expression in both — and the StatArb sleeve's claim to statistical significance no longer holds on the post-2011 sample alone.

## Metals (Copper, Aluminium, Lead, Zinc)

1. **On 2011-2026, Metals no longer shows a viable blended-strategy edge.** Momentum (-0.43), both Carry tenors (-0.10, -0.04), and both CarryMom tenors (-0.12, -0.06) are all negative full-sample, and even the equal-weight portfolio is negative (-0.12). Value is the sole positive strategy (+0.52). This is a materially weaker, more honest picture than the original 2006-2026 numbers, which were flattered by the 2008-09 crisis rally.

2. **The macro read hasn't changed, only sharpened**: this LME basket is a China-demand proxy first. The post-2011 sample captures the entire arc of China's slowdown from supercycle peak to today without the 2008-09 crisis distortion, and the complex simply has not offered a reliable systematic edge over that stretch outside of mean-reversion.

3. **A genuine "before and after 2016" split is visible in the Carry-family strategies that the old full-sample numbers hid.** Through the 2011-2015 supercycle unwind, Carry and CarryMom (F1-F3) were strongly positive (IR 0.75 and 0.77) even as metals prices fell — carry captures backwardation/contango, which stayed informative through a genuine, well-telegraphed bear market. Since 2016, every carry-family strategy has been negative in every regime. That reads as a decaying edge, not one that never worked.

4. **Momentum's full-sample failure is now unambiguous** (IR -0.43, the deepest drawdown in the sleeve at -124% cumulative). With the pre-crisis and crisis trending periods excluded, what's left is a market that has moved on discrete macro regime shifts (supercycle unwind, COVID, China slowdown) rather than persistent within-regime trends a moving-average crossover can harvest.

5. **Value's improvement versus the old full-sample number (IR 0.52 now vs. 0.40 before) is itself informative**: removing 2008-09, where Value posted the single worst cell in the old Metals grid (-1.48, a genuine crash-risk event for mean-reversion), actually helps its track record. Value has never needed a crisis to work — it just needed to not be caught in one.

6. **R2 (2016-2019) remains the dead zone across every strategy simultaneously** (Momentum -0.62, both Carry tenors below -0.8, both CarryMom tenors below -0.6, EW Portfolio -0.95) except Value (+0.76) — the clearest single-regime evidence that Value is the more durable construct once trend and carry lose their footing.

7. **R4 (2022-2026) shows the current-regime problem plainly**: every trend/carry strategy is negative, only Value is positive (+0.95) — consistent with China's property-and-infrastructure downturn removing the largest historical source of incremental base-metals demand, a demand-side story momentum and carry have no mechanism to anticipate.

8. **Tenor choice still matters more here than in any other complex**: the annual-convention tenor (F1-F13) is consistently the weaker of the two Carry variants in the post-2016 regimes specifically, reflecting Lead and Zinc's documented illiquidity at that point on the curve injecting noise exactly when there's no strong physical story to override it.

9. **Diversifying across the four strategies no longer clears a genuine bar here** — the equal-weight blend is negative full-sample (-0.12), only marginally better than the average standalone leg. A different conclusion from before: blending helped when three of four legs had real edges to combine; now that only Value does, equal-weighting mostly imports the other three legs' weakness at a lower volatility.

10. **Net call: Metals should be underweighted or expressed almost entirely through Value on the current evidence** — not through the equal-weight blend, and not through trend or carry, neither of which shows a reliable edge on this cleaner sample. Worth testing further: a carry-family strategy limited to pre-2016-style regimes (a regime-conditional carry overlay), given the strategy's genuine strength during the last sustained metals bear market.

## Oil / Energy (WTI, Brent, RBOB, Heating Oil, Nat Gas, Singapore Gasoil, Fuel Oil)

1. **Energy remains the strongest, most macro-coherent complex on the cleaner sample.** The equal-weight portfolio's full-sample IR (0.63) is still the best blended result of the four complexes, even after losing the boost the 2008-09 crisis gave the old full-sample number (0.80).

2. **Momentum is still the strongest standalone strategy** (IR 0.59), and the regime pattern is unchanged in character: geopolitical and event-driven shocks (2020 COVID, 2022 Russia-Ukraine) produce persistent, tradable direction; range-bound periods (2016-2019) do not.

3. **Value remains the one clear failure in the sleeve, and its case has arguably gotten worse on the cleaner sample** — still the only negative full-sample strategy (IR -0.09), and its 2011-2015 drawdown (-96%, on a -19% annualized return through the shale-driven collapse) is now visible as the dominant driver of its weak full-sample number, no longer partially offset by an earlier crisis-period gain that's outside the window.

4. **The far tenor (F1-F12) continues to outperform the near tenor (F1-F3) for both carry constructs** (Carry: 0.42 vs. 0.27; CarryMom: 0.61 vs. 0.34) — the term-structure risk premium in energy is priced more cleanly further out the curve, a pattern that holds up after removing the crisis-period data.

5. **R1 (2011-2015) is genuinely Energy's best "normal" regime once the crisis years are excluded** — every strategy except Value is solidly positive (Momentum 0.81, both Carry tenors above 0.65, CarryMom F1-F12 at 0.79). Unlike Metals, Energy's edge isn't purely a crisis-alpha story; it worked through an ordinary multi-year cycle too.

6. **R3 (COVID Shock) and 2026 YTD remain the standout windows for trend and carry-momentum** (Momentum IR 1.39 and 2.39 respectively), while Value stays negative or muted in both — the same shock-rewards-trend, punishes-value pattern holds up cleanly on the revised sample.

7. **R4 (2022-2026) is still broadly positive across every strategy** (Momentum 0.42, both Carry tenors 0.19-0.33, CarryMom 0.35-0.66, Value 0.45, EW Portfolio 0.66) — the post-Russia-Ukraine energy-security repricing continues to reward the whole sleeve simultaneously, not just one signal type.

8. **Value is now visibly a drag through nearly every regime in this sample, not just one bad crisis** — net negative through 2011-2015, roughly flat through 2016-2019, a drag through 2020-2021, only turning genuinely positive in the two most recent windows. The case for a Value-underweighted Energy variant is stronger on this cleaner sample than it was before.

9. **Return dispersion by regime remains the widest of any complex studied** (Momentum's return ranges from -1.5% in R2 to +110.7% in 2026 YTD) — regime classification still carries outsized value here relative to the other three sleeves.

10. **Net call: unchanged from the prior read — Energy is still the strongest, most defensible complex in the framework**, and the cleaner sample if anything reinforces the case for trimming or dropping Value in favor of trend and far-tenor carry-momentum.

## Precious Metals (Gold, Silver, Copper-COMEX, Platinum, Palladium)

1. **Precious Metals' picture has changed more than any other complex on the cleaner sample.** The original analysis called CarryMom the standout full-sample strategy (IR 0.34, beating the blend); on 2011-2026 it's much weaker (IR 0.07), and the equal-weight portfolio is now negative full-sample (-0.07). Almost all of CarryMom's old edge, and the portfolio's apparent diversification benefit, was concentrated in the 2008-09 window this sample no longer includes.

2. **What's left is a genuinely muted complex outside of the most recent window.** Value is now the (barely) strongest full-sample standalone strategy (IR 0.16), Carry is close behind (0.13), Momentum remains clearly negative (-0.38), and nothing in the sleeve shows a strong, reliable edge over the full 15-year sample.

3. **The one macro-relevant pattern that survives the revision is PM's continued divergence from the rest of the complex during shocks.** In the COVID Shock regime, Momentum, Carry, and Value are all negative even as trend/carry were strongly rewarded in Metals and Energy over the same window — gold and silver's initial COVID reaction was a liquidity-driven forced sale ahead of the subsequent stimulus rally, a whipsaw that specifically punishes trend and level-carry.

4. **Momentum's losing streak from 2011 through 2022-2026 is intact and, if anything, more visible now** that it isn't diluted by a strong pre-2011 period — a genuine decade-plus structural headwind (rising and then persistently elevated real rates, a strong dollar through most of the sample) rather than a tactical dip.

5. **The 2025-26 inflection is the clearest bright spot and stands out even more starkly against the weaker full-sample baseline**: Carry (IR 1.93), CarryMom (1.32), Value (1.15), and the blend (2.30) are all posting some of the strongest readings anywhere in the dataset — consistent with the well-covered contemporary gold/silver rally (Fed pivot expectations, central-bank buying, de-dollarization hedging).

6. **R1 (2011-2015) shows the post-2011 gold bear market plainly**: Momentum -0.37, Value barely positive (0.06), only Carry showing real strength (0.66) — a genuinely different regime character from Metals' same-period Carry strength, since PM's R1 weakness reflects a rates/dollar story rather than an industrial-demand one.

7. **R2 (2016-2019) is the one regime where Value clearly outperforms** (IR 0.91) while trend stays weak — the same calm-rewards-value pattern seen in Metals and Energy, though here it's the cleanest single regime in the whole PM sample where that pattern holds.

8. **Diversifying across the four strategies no longer earns its keep on this sample** — the equal-weight blend's full-sample IR (-0.07) actually trails every standalone strategy except Momentum. A stronger version of the original finding (there, EW at least beat Momentum and stayed positive); the four PM strategies are evidently correlated enough, dominated by the same real-rates/dollar factor, that blending doesn't manufacture an edge none of them individually has over most of this sample.

9. **The tight F1-F2 tenor choice still looks right on liquidity grounds, but Carry's modest full-sample edge (0.13) isn't translating into a strong blended result** — a conservative signal construction hasn't been enough on its own to produce a reliable full-sample return in this complex.

10. **Net call: Precious Metals does not currently support a strategic weight on its systematic full-sample numbers alone.** Value is the least-bad standalone expression, and the case for holding this sleeve right now rests on the live 2025-26 macro turn plus its ongoing role as a hedge against the shock regimes that hurt the other complexes — not on a persistent 15-year risk premium.

## NGL (Ethane, Propane, Butane, Isobutane, Ethylene, Propylene)

1. **NGL remains the strongest complex in the study on the cleaner sample** — Momentum (IR 1.25) and the equal-weight portfolio (IR 1.29) are both comfortably the best full-sample results across all four complexes. Unlike the original analysis, this number is no longer inflated by any burn-in artifact: the pipeline fix now lets each product's signals warm up on its own full history, moving Value's live-trading start from mid-2013 to February 2012.

2. **The one caveat that remains, and should stay explicit, is sample length rather than data quality.** NGL's own history starts in December 2010, so its "full sample" measured against 2011-2026 is credible and genuinely warmed-up — it's simply less battle-tested across market cycles than the other three complexes, whose usable history goes back further.

3. **Momentum's regime record is now the cleanest in the entire study**: positive in every single regime from R1 through 2026 YTD (0.66 to 2.03), the only strategy in any complex with a perfect record across every window it has data for.

4. **CarryMom is equally consistent, positive in all five windows** (0.61 to 1.43), reinforcing the cross-complex pattern that a rate-of-change signal on the curve outperforms the static level — and here more decisively than anywhere else (CarryMom full-sample IR 0.87 vs. Carry's 0.43, more than double).

5. **Carry (the static level construct) is the one clear weak spot, and its weakness is concentrated in the two most recent windows specifically** (R4: -0.97, 2026 YTD: -1.47) — a live, ongoing signal breakdown rather than a historical footnote, plausibly tied to structural changes in NGL export-capacity buildout altering the curve shape the F4-F15 convention was calibrated against.

6. **Value's regime pattern remains the most volatile of any strategy in the study**: a severe single-regime loss in 2011-2015 (IR -1.12, on a -18% annualized return) immediately followed by a run culminating in the single highest reading recorded anywhere in the dashboard (2026 YTD, IR 3.13). That still looks like a strategy that got run over once and has since ridden a favorable multi-year trend, not a stable structural edge — present the 2026 number as an outlier to watch.

7. **R3 (COVID Shock) remains NGL's standout regime across every strategy** (Momentum 2.03, Carry 1.58, CarryMom 1.43, EW Portfolio 2.25) — the same clean macro story as before: the negative-WTI print and the pandemic petrochemical demand collapse-then-snapback produced genuine, sustained curve dislocations.

8. **The equal-weight portfolio here (IR 1.29) is now, on a like-for-like 2011-2026 basis, clearly the single best blended result of any complex in the study** — ahead of Energy's 0.63 by a wide margin — a genuinely strong diversification outcome that holds up on the cleaner sample.

9. **The tenor convention (F4-F15) remains unique to NGL and still isn't directly comparable to the other complexes' near-tenor Carry pairs** — cross-complex claims should stay scoped to Momentum, CarryMom, and the blended portfolio, where construction is consistent enough to compare.

10. **Net call: NGL is now the highest-conviction sleeve in the framework, not just the highest-uncertainty one.** The burn-in fix removed the main data-quality objection from the original analysis, and the regime-by-regime consistency — especially Momentum's perfect record — is a genuinely strong result. The remaining caveat is simply that NGL's track record spans one full commodity cycle rather than several; present it as the strongest current evidence for the framework's thesis while being explicit about that shorter history.
