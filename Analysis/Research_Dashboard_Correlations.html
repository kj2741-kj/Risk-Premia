Risk Premia Correlation Matrices

Companion to Research_Dashboard_CombinedCarry.html. Daily NET log-return correlations, 2011-01-01 to 2026-06-30.

Methodology: all correlations are Pearson, computed on daily NET log-return series (the same 6 series -- Momentum, Carry, CarryMom, Value, EW PORT, Risk Parity -- underlying Research_Dashboard_CombinedCarry.html), sliced to the standardized reporting window (2011-01-01 onward, same as every other dashboard in this project). Within-asset-class matrices use each asset class's own trading calendar directly (no gaps, since every strategy series is already reindexed to the common product window and filled to 0 on non-trading days). Cross-commodity matrices align the four asset classes on the INTERSECTION of trading dates only (n=3,828 days) -- Metals (LME), Oil/Energy (NYMEX/ICE), Precious Metals (COMEX/NYMEX), and NGL (Argus OTC) trade on different calendars, so an inner join is the only way to compare them fairly rather than silently treating "exchange closed" as "flat that day" (same convention already documented in research/engine.py::combine_returns).

Within-Asset-Class: Momentum, Carry, CarryMom, Value, EW PORT, Risk Parity

How correlated are the 4 base strategies and 2 portfolio combinations with EACH OTHER, inside one asset class? High EW/Risk Parity correlation with individual strategies indicates concentration risk (the "portfolio" isn't diversifying much); negative Value correlation with the other three strategies is the diversification benefit Value is supposed to provide.

Cross-Commodity: same strategy, across all 4 asset classes

How correlated is (say) Metals Momentum with Energy Momentum, Precious Metals Momentum, and NGL Momentum? High cross-commodity correlation for a given strategy/portfolio row means that row is NOT providing independent diversification across asset classes -- worth knowing before sizing multiple asset classes together at the portfolio level.

strong positive (≥0.5) weak positive (0.15-0.5) near zero (<0.15) weak negative (0.15-0.5) strong negative (≥0.5)

Key patterns

Energy and NGL are not independent books. Their cross-commodity correlation is the highest off-diagonal pair for 5 of 6 strategy types -- Value (0.53), Momentum (0.45), Risk Parity (0.31), EW PORT (0.28), Carry (0.23) -- confirming both trade on shared hydrocarbon-complex drivers (shale/export capacity cycles, the 2022 energy shock). Sizing both asset classes up together should account for this shared exposure, not treat them as two fully independent diversifiers.

Metals and Precious Metals show a smaller but consistent correlation across every strategy type (~0.30-0.36 for Momentum, Carry, Value, EW PORT, and Risk Parity) -- both being "metals" in a broad sense, this is a real, if modest, shared-factor exposure worth knowing about even though the two complexes have very different fundamental drivers (industrial demand vs. real rates).

CarryMom is the most genuinely diversifying strategy across commodities -- its cross-commodity correlations are the lowest of any row type (mostly under 0.16 in magnitude, several near zero or slightly negative), suggesting term-structure rate-of-change carries little shared information across unrelated commodity complexes, unlike Value and Momentum which both show meaningful cross-commodity correlation.

Within every asset class, Value is the true diversifier -- it is the only strategy with a negative or near-zero correlation to EW PORT in 3 of 4 asset classes (Metals 0.04, Energy -0.19, NGL -0.02; only Precious Metals breaks the pattern at 0.37), consistent with the mean-reversion vs. trend-following split already visible in the regime tables. Risk Parity is consistently MORE correlated with EW PORT than any individual strategy (0.78-0.96 across all four asset classes) -- expected, since both portfolios are built from the same 4 legs, just weighted differently.
