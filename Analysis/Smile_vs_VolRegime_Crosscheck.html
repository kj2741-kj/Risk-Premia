Does the Momentum Smile Agree with the Vol-Regime Study? DOESN'T HOLD UNIVERSALLY

The momentum smile says momentum is rewarded by big moves. The earlier vol-regime study classified every day as Low/Medium/High realized volatility and measured Momentum's Sharpe in each. Naive prediction: if big moves help momentum, Momentum's Sharpe should be highest in the High vol regime, for every product, and products with a steeper smile should show a bigger High-vs-Low Sharpe gap. Neither held up cleanly.

Only 7 of 20 products even show the directionally-expected result (High vol beats Low vol for Momentum), and only 1 is strictly monotonic. The correlation between how steep a product's smile is and how much its Momentum Sharpe actually improves in high vol is essentially zero (-0.07, not significant) — smile steepness tells you almost nothing about the vol-regime effect.

Why they diverge — the resolution

The two studies measure genuinely different things. The smile's x-axis is net directional return over a month — a trend. The vol-regime study's "High" bucket is realized volatility — the magnitude of day-to-day price swings, regardless of direction. A month can be high-volatility and still net out near zero if the swings are choppy and offsetting (a whipsaw month) — which is exactly the smile's worst case for momentum, even though it would land in the vol study's "High" bucket. High vol is necessary for a big trending move but doesn't guarantee one; it can just as easily mean elevated chop. That gap between "volatile" and "trending" is what breaks the naive prediction, and explains why it survives in Energy (where high-vol periods are more often genuine directional dislocations — supply shocks, OPEC moves) but fails in NGL (where high-vol periods look more like seasonal/roll-driven noise than sustained trends).
